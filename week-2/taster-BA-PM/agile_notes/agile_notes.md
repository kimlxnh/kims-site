## WATERFALL VS. AGILE

In the **waterfall approach** to project management, the client and business analyst collaborate to develop a project specification, which is then passed down to the delivery team tasked with project execution.

**Benefits** of **waterfall**: 
- **Clear structure** - waterfall methodology offers a distinct and clear structure in contrast to other project management approaches, as it focusses on a well-defined sequence of steps
- **Limited scope** - the rigidity of the waterfall model is advantageous in scenarios where the project scope is narrow and precisely defined, as it makes changes more challenging

**Challenges** of **waterfall**: 
- **Limited customer engagement** - the lack of continuous interaction with the customer throughout the software development lifecycle (SDLC) increases the risk of delivering a final product that diverges from the client's initial vision
- **Outdated product** - there's a risk of the product becoming outdated or not aligning with current market trends after six months to a year of production, potentially rendering it redundant

The rigid, sequential nature of **waterfall** can pose significant challenges, especially in today's dynamic and fast-paced environment.

In response to these challenges, the Agile methodology emerged as a transformative (and more responsive) approach to software development, as it prioritise adaptability, collaboration, and customer-centricity.

## AGILE

<div class="r-stretch">
    <img src="https://miro.medium.com/proxy/1*qpD1rt8_od8y-x-Uq_pwag.png" />
</div>

Agile working was developed to meet the challenges of waterfall management.

Agile project management closely involves the client in all stages of the project lifecycle, not just the build. The involvement of the client ensures that the project delivery team 'fails fast, but fails cheaply'.

The delivery team first creates a minimum viable product of the project, and then demos the product, and constantly improves the product based on client feedback.

They are constantly showing working software to the client, at fixed periods, so that they can change the course of the project development.

This process leads to a finished product that the client has full ownership of and involvement in, and is created without wastage of money and time due to errors.

### AGILE IS NOT

- A methodology
- A specific way of developing software
- A framework or process

### AGILE IS

- A set of values and principles

## Agile Manifesto

[Agile Manifesto](https://agilemanifesto.org/)

<br>

### VALUES

- Individuals and interactions OVER processes and tools
- Working software OVER comprehensive documentation
- Customer collaboration OVER contract negotiation
- Responding to changes OVER following a plan

1. Having the right group of people, **the right team**, is vital to success. The best tools in the wrong hands are worthless. **Communication** and **collaboration** of your team is key
2. **Documentation** is clearly a good thing BUT there comes a point where you need to focus on providing your customers with **good, working, software**
3. Once upon a time, contracts were king. You would draw up contracts with your customers who would then detail the finished product. As a result, there was often a contrast between what the contract said, what the product did, and what the customer actually required. Focus HAS to be on **continuous development**
4. The trouble with static roadmaps is that we don’t live in a static world. Needs and requirements are always shifting, and priorities are always changing. That static roadmap will soon grow outdated

This is why the **Agile Manifesto** suggests that a software team should have the ability to pivot and change direction whenever they need to, with a flexible roadmap that reflects that.


### PRINCIPLES

1. The highest priority is to satisfy the customer through early and continuous delivery of valuable software
2. Welcome changing requirements even late in development. Agile processes harness change for the customer's competitive advantage
3. Deliver working software frequently from a couple of weeks to a couple of months, with a preference to the shorter timescale
4. Business people and developers must work together daily throughout the project
5. Build projects around motivated individuals. give them the environment they need and trust them to get the job done
6. The most efficient and effective method of conveying information to and within a dev team is face-to-face conversation
7. Working software is the primary measure of progress
8. Agile processes promote sustainable development. The sponsors, developers and users should be able to maintain a constant pace indefinitely
9. Continuous attention to technical exellence and good design enhances agility
10. Simplicity: the art of maximising the amount of work NOT DONE is essential
11. The best architectures, requirements, and designs emerge from self-organising teams
12. At regular intervals, the team reflects on how to become more effective, then tunes and adjusts its behaviour accordingly

## WHAT IS SCRUM

Scrum is one of the most widely used frameworks within the Agile methodology, as it provides a structured framework for managing complex projects.

The Agile Scrum practice relies on incremental development where projects are broken down into smaller, manageable chunks of work called increments, delivered over iterative cycles known as sprints.

Each iteration consists of 1-4 week sprints, where the goal of each sprint is to build the most important features first and come out with a potentially deliverable product.

Scrum is a lightweight framework that helps people, teams and organizations generate value through adaptive solutions for complex problems. It is simple to implement but very difficult to master. Hence we have someone dedicated to the scrum team who ensures that the values and processes of Scrum are followed well in the team.

### SCRUM TEAM

1. DEVELOPERS
2. PRODUCT OWNER
3. SCRUM MASTER

- **Developers**: Devs are the people in the Scrum Team that are committed to creating any aspect of a usable working piece of software as part of the delivery of each increment or sprint
- **Product Owner**: The Product Owner is accountable for maximising the value of the product resulting from the work of the Scrum Team. The Product Owner is one person, not a team. Their decisions are respected by the entire organisation. The PO represents the needs of many stakeholders and is accountable for the delivery of the product
- **Scrum Master**: The Scrum Master is accountable for the Scrum Team's effectiveness. They do this by enabling the Scrum Team to improve its practices, within the Scrum framework. But at the end of the day they are servant leaders. They do not have authority over anyone

A successful Scrum team is one where everyone is empowered and self-managed. They also need to be cross-functional.

Scrum teams are generally made up of 10 people or fewer for better communication purposes. Bigger teams are generally broken down into smaller Scrum teams but still sharing the same Product Backlog, Product Goal and Product Owner.

### PILLARS OF SCRUM

1. TRANSPARENCY
2. INSPECTION
3. ADAPTATION

**Transparency** - the process and work should be visible to all of the stakeholders, whether they are within the team or external. Everyone should be told about the good news as well as the bad news. The scrum artifacts will help to share information with high transparency

- **From Scrum guide: Transparency enables inspection. Inspection without transparency is misleading and wasteful.**

**Inspection** - here there is no special inspector coming in and doing the inspection. In fact, it is everyone's responsibility to inspect the agreed goals against the artifacts and the progress of the project to detect issues

- **From Scrum guide : Inspection enables adaptation. Inspection without adaptation is considered pointless.**

**Adaptation** - if anything during the progress of the project deviates from the permissible limits, then the process being applied or the product being produced must be adjusted ASAP to minimize further deviation. Based on the results of inspection, adaptation needs to take place

### SCRUM CEREMONIES
1. SPRINT PLANNING
2. DAILY STANDUP
3. SPRINT REVIEW
4. SPRINT RETROSPECTIVE

- **Sprint planning**
Sprint planning initiates the Sprint by laying out the work to be performed for the Sprint. The plan is created by the collaborative work of the entire Scrum Team. The Product Owner ensures that attendees are prepared to discuss the most important Product Backlog items and how they map to the Product Goal.

- **Daily Standup**
The Daily Standup or Daily Scrum is a 15-minute event for the developers of the Scrum Team. To reduce complexity, it is held at the same time and place every working day of the Sprint.

- **Sprint Review (also known as 'Show and Tell')**
The purpose of the Sprint Review is a chance to bring the team together, helping stakeholders and others bond with the team by sharing success and improving collaboration.

- **Sprint Retrospective**
The purpose of the Sprint Retro is to plan ways to increase quality and effectiveness. The Scrum Team inspects how the last Sprint went w.r.t. individuals, interactions, processes, tools and their Definition of Done. The Scrum Team discusses what went well, what problems it encountered, and how those problems were (or were not) addressed.

**Product Backlog** - a prioritised order of everything that might be developed in a product or service. Often this list is formed by user stories that focus on what a user might be able to do.

The job of the sprint is to deliver selected features from the backlog. The objective is to produce valuable product increments from each sprint.

- **WIP Limit** - Work in Progress -> the number of tasks a team are prepared to have on the go at any one point. This is set by the Scrum Master. _Remember, the purpose of the WIP is to ensure no-one takes too much on and to reduce bottlenecks_
- **Column** - the stage that a particular task is at
- **Card** - an individual task or job
- **Swimlane** - Kanban swimlanes are horizontal lines that split a Kanban board into sections. Teams usually use them to visually separate different work types on the same board and organize homogenous tasks together

A Kanban board is different than a Scrum board because of the WIP Limit.

### SCRUM ARTIFACTS

1. PRODUCT BACKLOG
2. SPRINT BACKLOG
3. THE INCREMENT

An artifact is something that is physically produced.

- **Product Backlog** - contains the Product Goal as well as the total items that need to be done for the product to be deemed complete
- **Sprint Backlog** - the work to be done in that particular sprint
- **Increment** - the working software produced in every sprint is called the increment. The sum of all the increments is the final intended outcome for the project to be deemed completed
